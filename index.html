Thermodynamic & Numerical Foundations

Engineering Derivation: Non-Ideal VLE & Rachford-Rice Solution

Mathematical formulation of liquid activity coefficients, vapor-liquid phase equilibrium, and monotonic Newton-Raphson convergence criteria.

1. Phase Equilibrium & Modified Raoult's Law

For a non-ideal binary mixture of Ethanol (1) and Water (2), vapor-liquid equilibrium requires chemical potential equality for every species (fiV = fiL):

yi · P = xi · γi(T, x) · Pisat(T) · Φicorr ⇒ Ki(T, P, x) ≡ yixi = γi(T, x) · Pisat(T)P

where Pisat(T) is calculated from the Antoine vapor pressure relation:

log10(Pisat [bar]) = Ai − BiT [K] + Ci
2. Wilson Liquid Activity Coefficient Model

The Wilson equation accurately represents polar azeotropic mixtures without liquid-liquid phase separation:

ln γ1 = −ln(x1 + Λ12x2) + x2 [ Λ12x1 + Λ12x2 − Λ21Λ21x1 + x2 ]
ln γ2 = −ln(Λ21x1 + x2) − x1 [ Λ12x1 + Λ12x2 − Λ21Λ21x1 + x2 ]

where Λij is defined by molar liquid volumes and characteristic binary interaction energies:

Λij = VjLViL · exp [ − aijR · T ]
a12 = 437.98 cal/mol a21 = 1256.41 cal/mol V1L = 58.68 cm3/mol V2L = 18.07 cm3/mol
3. The Rachford-Rice Objective Function

Combining overall and component material balances yields the monotonic Rachford-Rice objective function in terms of vapor fraction β = V/F:

r(β) = ∑Ci=1 zi · (Ki − 1)1 + β · (Ki − 1) = 0
First Analytical Derivative (Strict Monotonicity & Uniqueness):
r'(β) = − ∑Ci=1 zi · (Ki − 1)2[1 + β · (Ki − 1)]2 < 0

Because r'(β) < 0 everywhere on [0, 1], the Newton-Raphson iteration βn+1 = βn − r(βn) / r'(βn) exhibits quadratic convergence without numerical oscillation.

Industrial Scaling Bottlenecks • Engineering Analysis

Why Single-Unit Solvers Cannot Transfer Directly to Industrial Plants

A critical examination of tear-stream recycles, stiff DAE systems, non-convex phase envelopes, and simulator latency in full-scale commercial flowsheets.

1. Recycle Loops & Tear-Stream Numerical Divergence

In single-unit flash separation, flow is strictly feed-forward. Industrial chemical plants (e.g. ammonia, methanol, ethylene) recycle unreacted synthesis gas back to reactor inlets:

  • Commercial simulators use numerical accelerators (Wegstein, Broyden, or Newton-Raphson) to converge tear streams.
  • When an AI agent modifies an upstream parameter, the recycle loop often enters limit cycles or diverges. Single-unit solvers have zero tear-stream sensitivity.
2. Stiff Differential-Algebraic Equations (DAEs) in Multi-Stage Equipment

Distillation columns and packed absorbers couple 50+ stages of non-linear MESH equations:

  • The resulting Jacobian matrix condition number κ(J) frequently exceeds 10^12.
  • Unconstrained AI parameter exploration causes stiff solver breakdown and matrix singularities.
3. Non-Convex Gibbs Energy Minimization & False Local Minima

Non-ideal mixtures with azeotropes exhibit non-convex Gibbs energy surfaces (ΔG_mix). Simplified flash algorithms can fall into false local minima, predicting homogeneous single-phase states when a two-liquid (LLE/VLLE) phase split occurs.

4. Computational Latency Gap: 1 ms vs. 45 Seconds

Our analytical flash solver executes in 0.4 milliseconds. In contrast, converging a 25-unit industrial flowsheet in Aspen Plus or DWSIM requires 5 to 45 seconds per iteration, creating severe throughput bottlenecks for real-time reinforcement learning.

Commercial Strategy • Product Commercialization

From Headless Simulator to Ambient Assistive Copilot

How single-unit flowsheet automation serves as the foundational validation engine for an enterprise platform that learns complex, non-text engineering workflows and delivers proactive, human-in-the-loop assistance.

PILLAR 1: How Headless Simulation Leads to Assistive Tech Building

Current developer assistants (GitHub Copilot, Cursor) have transformed software engineering because code is structured, AST-parseable, and bounded by formal compiler syntaxes. Conversely, technical and industrial engineering software (Aspen Plus, DWSIM, ChemCAD, AutoCAD, SolidWorks, and industrial mold design) remains largely unassisted:

  • High Setup Burden: Before running a single simulation, an engineer must choose from 50+ property packages, enter binary parameters, specify stream enthalpies, and connect unit ports manually.
  • The 3-Phase Lifecycle: Passive Observation (Sessions 1 to 3) • Calibrated Suggestion (Sessions 4 to 10) • Proactive Execution (Sessions 10+).
  • Why Chemical Engineering First? Actions are governed by strict physical conservation laws (mass, energy, momentum) and non-linear thermodynamics. Proving reliability here allows the platform to generalize horizontally to CAD, CAM, and corporate multi-app operations.
PILLAR 2: What All Needs to Be Figured Out (Core Research Challenges)
  • 1. Telemetry vs. Vision in Legacy GUIs: Legacy engineering software runs on Win32, COM, or custom C++ canvas renderers where accessibility trees are often empty. We must engineer a hybrid observer combining OS accessibility hooks with real-time screen-diffing computer vision (< 50 ms latency).
  • 2. Dynamic Flowsheet Knowledge Graph: Text tokens cannot capture stream topologies. We must develop Flowsheet Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) that encode unit blocks, connectivity matrices, phase envelopes, and user intent as an evolving topological state vector.
  • 3. Self-Blurring Enterprise IP Privacy: Chemical flowsheets contain proprietary catalyst recipes and plant P&IDs. The platform requires an on-device Self-Blurring engine that masks confidential coordinates, formulas, and passwords before local inference.
  • 4. Multi-Tenant Governance: Reconciling personal operator habits with corporate engineering standards via federated LoRA adapters.
  • 5. Physics-Informed Safety & Non-Disruption: Every action must be verified against an analytical thermodynamic envelope before dispatching, coupled with transactional rollback (instant Ctrl+Z).
PILLAR 3: Required Technical Capabilities & System Architecture
  • Layer 1 (Perception): Windows UI Automation (UIA3), Win32 event hooks, lightweight OpenCV frame-differencing, and OCR text extraction.
  • Layer 2 (Context Graph): Flowsheet Graph Neural Network (GNN) maintaining directed bipartite flowsheet graph (Streams ↔ Unit Blocks), coupled with SQLite vector memory.
  • Layer 3 (Intent Model): Fine-tuned local Small Language Model (SLM, 8B Q4 quantized) with Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for multi-step task planning.
  • Layer 4 (Safety Filter): Analytical Wilson/NRTL thermodynamic solvers, Souders-Brown equipment flooding limiters, and mass balance conservation auditors.
  • Layer 5 (UI Overlay): Rust/Tauri lightweight transparent desktop overlay, ghost autocomplete rendering, and COM/SendInput hardware dispatch.
