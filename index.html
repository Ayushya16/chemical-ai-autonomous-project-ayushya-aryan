Thermodynamic & Numerical Foundations

Engineering Derivation: Non-Ideal VLE & Rachford-Rice Solution

Mathematical formulation of liquid activity coefficients, vapor-liquid phase equilibrium, and monotonic Newton-Raphson convergence criteria.

1. Phase Equilibrium & Modified Raoult's Law

For a non-ideal binary mixture of Ethanol (1) and Water (2), vapor-liquid equilibrium requires chemical potential equality for every species (fiV = fiL):

yi · P = xi · γi(T, x) · Pisat(T) · Φicorr ⇒ Ki(T, P, x) ≡ yixi = γi(T, x) · Pisat(T)P

where Pisat(T) is calculated from the Antoine vapor pressure relation:

log10(Pisat [bar]) = Ai − BiT [K] + Ci
2. Wilson Liquid Activity Coefficient Model

The Wilson equation accurately represents polar azeotropic mixtures without liquid-liquid phase separation:

ln γ1 = −ln(x1 + Λ12x2) + x2 [ Λ12x1 + Λ12x2 − Λ21Λ21x1 + x2 ]
ln γ2 = −ln(Λ21x1 + x2) − x1 [ Λ12x1 + Λ12x2 − Λ21Λ21x1 + x2 ]

where Λij is defined by molar liquid volumes and characteristic binary interaction energies:

Λij = VjLViL · exp [ − aijR · T ]
a12 = 437.98 cal/mol a21 = 1256.41 cal/mol V1L = 58.68 cm3/mol V2L = 18.07 cm3/mol
3. The Rachford-Rice Objective Function

Combining overall and component material balances yields the monotonic Rachford-Rice objective function in terms of vapor fraction β = V/F:

r(β) = ∑Ci=1 zi · (Ki − 1)1 + β · (Ki − 1) = 0
First Analytical Derivative (Strict Monotonicity & Uniqueness):
r'(β) = − ∑Ci=1 zi · (Ki − 1)2[1 + β · (Ki − 1)]2 < 0
4. Aspen HYSYS Engine: Peng-Robinson Equation of State (PR-EOS)

For high-pressure multi-component hydrocarbon systems (natural gas mixtures containing C1, C2, C3, nC4, nC5+), Aspen HYSYS solves the cubic Peng-Robinson Equation of State:

P = R · Tv − b − a(T)v(v + b) + b(v − b)
Compressibility Factor Cubic Formulation:
Z3 − (1 − B)Z2 + (A − 3B2 − 2B)Z − (AB − B2 − B3) = 0

where dimensionless parameters A and B are defined using critical properties (Tc,i, Pc,i) and acentric factor ωi:

A = a · P(R · T)2,   B = b · PR · T,   αi(Tr,i) = [ 1 + mi · (1 − √Tr,i) ]2
mi = 0.37464 + 1.54226 · ωi − 0.26992 · ωi2

Classical van der Waals quadratic mixing rules establish mixture attractive and co-volume parameters:

a = ∑Ci=1 ∑Cj=1 zizj √aiaj (1 − kij),   b = ∑Ci=1 zibi

Phase equilibrium criteria dictate equal component fugacities (fiV = fiL), yielding equilibrium K-values directly from the PR fugacity coefficient φi:

Ki = φiL(T, P, x)φiV(T, P, y)
Retrograde Condensation Physics: In high-pressure gas reservoirs near the cricondenbar (70–84 bar), isothermal depressurization causes the mixture to cross the dew-point boundary from the single-phase vapor into the two-phase region. Rather than expanding vapor, liquid condensate paradoxically drops out of the gas stream due to non-ideal dense fluid molecular interactions captured by PR-EOS.
Industrial Scaling Bottlenecks • Engineering Analysis

Why Single-Unit Solvers Cannot Transfer Directly to Industrial Plants

A critical examination of tear-stream recycles, stiff DAE systems, non-convex phase envelopes, and simulator latency in full-scale commercial flowsheets.

1. Recycle Loops & Tear-Stream Numerical Divergence

In single-unit flash separation, flow is strictly feed-forward. Industrial chemical plants (e.g. ammonia, methanol, ethylene) recycle unreacted synthesis gas back to reactor inlets:

  • Commercial simulators use numerical accelerators (Wegstein, Broyden, or Newton-Raphson) to converge tear streams.
  • When an AI agent modifies an upstream parameter, the recycle loop often enters limit cycles or diverges. Single-unit solvers have zero tear-stream sensitivity.
2. Stiff Differential-Algebraic Equations (DAEs) in Multi-Stage Equipment

Distillation columns and packed absorbers couple 50+ stages of non-linear MESH equations:

  • The resulting Jacobian matrix condition number κ(J) frequently exceeds 10^12.
  • Unconstrained AI parameter exploration causes stiff solver breakdown and matrix singularities.
3. Non-Convex Gibbs Energy Minimization & False Local Minima

Non-ideal mixtures with azeotropes exhibit non-convex Gibbs energy surfaces (ΔG_mix). Simplified flash algorithms can fall into false local minima, predicting homogeneous single-phase states when a two-liquid (LLE/VLLE) phase split occurs.

4. Computational Latency Gap: 1 ms vs. 45 Seconds

Our analytical flash solver executes in 0.4 milliseconds. In contrast, converging a 25-unit industrial flowsheet in Aspen Plus or DWSIM requires 5 to 45 seconds per iteration, creating severe throughput bottlenecks for real-time reinforcement learning.

Non-Technical Summary for Stakeholders
💡 Part 1: What is the Product? (Think "GitHub Copilot for Desktop Engineering")

If you write software code, tools like GitHub Copilot or Cursor watch what you type, guess what function you want next, and write it for you with one click. Our product does the exact same thing, but for heavy desktop engineering software—starting with chemical plant simulators like Aspen HYSYS and DWSIM, and eventually expanding to CAD/CAM tools like AutoCAD and SolidWorks.

Currently, setting up a chemical process simulation is agonizingly manual: an engineer must click through dozens of deep legacy menus, pick from over 50 confusing thermodynamic equations, type in hundreds of temperature/pressure values by hand, and wire up complex pipelines. One wrong choice causes the mathematical solver to crash with cryptic error codes.

How our ambient copilot works in day-to-day practice:

  • Ambient Background Observer: It quietly runs as a lightweight sidecar on the engineer's desktop, watching mouse clicks, open windows, and flowsheet connections without getting in the way.
  • Intent Inference & Smart Autocomplete: When an engineer places a flash drum or distillation column on the canvas, the AI infers what they are trying to achieve (e.g., "separating ethanol from water") and proactively recommends the exact thermodynamic model (e.g., Wilson/NRTL) and operating parameters that will guarantee numerical convergence.
  • Human-in-the-Loop Confirmation: The AI never takes unilateral control of the mouse or executes blind changes. Instead, it offers a translucent preview badge (like a ghost code completion). The engineer reviews the suggested inputs and presses Tab or clicks once to apply them instantly.
  • Physical Pre-Check: Before dispatching any input into the simulator, our headless physics engine runs an ultra-fast verification in the background to ensure mass and energy balances are conserved and no physical hazard (like an overpressurized vessel) is created.
⚠️ Part 2: Why Can't It Be Built to a Full Scale Yet? (The 5 Fundamental Roadblocks)

Building an AI copilot for text code is relatively straightforward because code is plain text with open compilers and millions of open-source repositories on GitHub. Building an AI copilot for complex desktop engineering systems faces severe physical, technical, and data barriers that require rigorous phased R&D:

  • 1. Legacy GUI Black Boxes (No Modern Web APIs): Commercial engineering tools like Aspen Plus, HYSYS, and ChemCAD were built 20 to 30 years ago on legacy Windows Win32/C++ architectures. They do not have modern web DOM trees or clean REST APIs. To "see" what an engineer is doing, we have to invent hybrid perception systems that combine operating system accessibility hooks with real-time computer vision screen scraping.
  • 2. Real-World Physical Catastrophe vs. Harmless Code Bugs: If GitHub Copilot makes a mistake in Python code, your script throws a harmless error and you delete the line. In chemical plants, refineries, and nuclear facilities, an incorrect temperature, wrong pressure rating, or invalid activity package in the design can cause physical column flooding, equipment explosions, or millions of dollars in plant downtime. The AI cannot "hallucinate"—every single recommendation must be bounded by rigid physical conservation laws.
  • 3. Proprietary Corporate Data & Zero Public Datasets: GitHub has billions of lines of open-source code for training AI models. In industrial engineering, chemical flowsheets, proprietary catalyst recipes, and plant P&IDs are closely guarded corporate trade secrets. There is no public dataset of 500,000 industrial plant flowsheets. Training data must be generated synthetically via automated headless simulation sweeps or learned privately on-premise.
  • 4. The 60-Second Simulator Latency Trap: An interactive copilot must respond in under 100 milliseconds to feel intuitive. While our single-unit flash separator prototype solves in 0.4 milliseconds, a real-world industrial plant flowsheet with 30 unit blocks and recycle loops takes 10 to 60+ seconds to converge in Aspen. An AI cannot explore trial actions in real time on the actual commercial simulator without first building ultra-fast neural network surrogate approximations.
  • 5. Air-Gapped Security & Intellectual Property Protection: Chemical conglomerates and manufacturing plants operate inside air-gapped, isolated networks without internet access to prevent industrial espionage. We cannot simply send plant diagrams to cloud APIs like OpenAI or Claude. The entire intelligence engine, computer vision stack, and physics verifier must run fully locally on modest desktop hardware.
PILLAR 1: How Headless Simulation Leads to Assistive Tech Building

Current developer assistants (GitHub Copilot, Cursor) have transformed software engineering because code is structured, AST-parseable, and bounded by formal compiler syntaxes. Conversely, technical and industrial engineering software (Aspen Plus, DWSIM, ChemCAD, AutoCAD, SolidWorks, and industrial mold design) remains largely unassisted:

  • High Setup Burden: Before running a single simulation, an engineer must choose from 50+ property packages, enter binary parameters, specify stream enthalpies, and connect unit ports manually.
  • The 3-Phase Lifecycle: Passive Observation (Sessions 1 to 3) • Calibrated Suggestion (Sessions 4 to 10) • Proactive Execution (Sessions 10+).
  • Why Chemical Engineering First? Actions are governed by strict physical conservation laws (mass, energy, momentum) and non-linear thermodynamics. Proving reliability here allows the platform to generalize horizontally to CAD, CAM, and corporate multi-app operations.
